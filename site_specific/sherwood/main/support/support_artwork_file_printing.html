What is transparency?

In the printing/digital world, transparency has a broader meaning. Blending mode (other than Normal), opacity less than 100%, opacity mask, drop shadows, feathering, inner glow or outer glow effect, etc. are all related to transparency. There are two forms of transparency: live transparency and flattened transparency. Due to the fact that live transparency often causes printing issues, live transparency needs to be flattened before printing. “Flattening” is a process of converting live transparency into a format that does not support live transparency, i.e... opaque objects are generated to simulate transparent effects in original files. The new opaque objects are so-called “flattened transparency”. As artwork becomes complex, so does the flattening and its results. For example, white lines can be seen at the stitch between new opaque zones. Colors may be shifted when different color spaces or color modes are used to create transparency. You can apply the flattening in several ways. Here are three simple methods: Select the objects that require flattening and choose Object→Flatten Transparency. Choose a default setting or a custom preset (that you created) from the Preset drop-down list and click OK. Choose File→Print and select Advanced from the list of print options on the left. Choose a preset from the Overprint and Transparency Flattener options. If you used the Attributes panel to create overprints (for trapping used in high-end printing), make sure to preserve the overprints. Overprints aren't preserved in areas that use transparency. Choose File→Save As and choose Illustrator EPS. In the Transparency section of the EPS Options dialog box, choose a flattening setting from the Preset drop-down list. If your transparency options are grayed out, your file has no transparency.

What is overprint? What are the pros and cons and when to use overprint?

When you print opaque, overlapping colors, the top color knocks out the area underneath. Overprinting prevents knockouts and makes the topmost overlapping printing ink appear transparent in relation to the underlying ink. Set up overprinting: Select the object or objects that you want to overprint. In the Attributes panel, select Overprint Fill, Overprint Stroke, or both.

What should be color mode of my files?

CMYK on process colour jobs, Grey scale on black only and right Pantone colours to be used in file depends on type of print. RGB colour will give you unexpected results and hence not accepted as standard.

How to prepare file to avoid purple tone where I need blue colour?

Purple tone happens because of Magenta contents in file. Usually 100% Cyan and 100% Magenta is used by designers. Instead use 100% Cyan and 70 to 80% of Magenta for better results.

What is bleed and crop marks?

Bleed is the area of artwork that is extended beyond the actual dimensions of the document. It is used to avoid strips of white paper showing on the edges of your print should the batch be misaligned when cut to size. Any objects in your artwork that touch the edges of the document require bleed, for instance a background colour or image should spread to cover the entire bleed area as should any objects that creep in from the side of the page. Crop marks, also known as trim marks, are lines printed in the corners of your publication's sheet or sheets of paper to show the printer where to trim the paper.

What is rich black and where can I use it?

Usually in larger areas of solid black we recommend to use C=60%, M=40%, Y=40% and K=100% to maintain strength of deep black. Do not use these settings in text as adversely affect the results.

What resolution should my file be?

300 DPI. We recommend you to avoid Photoshop files for small text. Vector art always give you better result on text.

What kind of ink percentage should we avoid to use?

Do not use anything over 300% of ink coverage as it affect in drying the ink on press and also creates flakes on edge on sides after cutting.

What happens if there is border on the job?

All care will be taken in producing your job throughout print, bind and trimming process but still because of limitation of the process you might see variance in job with border. Watch your artwork to avoid unwanted results.

How do I send files for printing with custom size, special finishing or die cut?

We recommend you to include three files for this type of special attention jobs. File 1 - Combination of print and die line for reference File 2 – Print only file for printing File 3 – Die line only file for making die, layout etc. Also submit mock up for this type of jobs. Having mock up always helps eliminating guess work.

How to set up my file for proper back up?

Generally two sided jobs are printed back to back if they have same orientation on both sides. Like portrait job will be flipped along long edge and landscape job will be flipped along short edge. File having different orientation on both sides will be printed as per our discursion unless clearly specified by you.
